# Issue 041 — too much negative space on the Montrose lawns

- **Reported by:** owner playtest 2026-08-02 (screenshot shown in chat, not saved to
  disk — REPRODUCE it from the description below with `tools/shot.mjs`, the standing
  no-bug-reports-file pattern)
- **Area:** montrose
- **Severity:** MEDIUM-HIGH — this is a *feel* problem on one of the four
  neighborhoods the owner is holding to an AAA bar before any expansion.
- **Owner's words:** *"too much space in this area, let's minimize 'negative space'."*
- **Routed to:** task 129.

## The view he was looking at

Standing on the lakefront **bike path** (lavender asphalt with yellow centre dashes)
where it runs diagonally across the Montrose lawns, camera facing roughly north/
north-west toward **Cricket Hill**:

- The entire left two-thirds of the frame is **unbroken flat green lawn** — no trees,
  no props, no people, no path furniture, nothing between the bike path and the hill.
  Only a few scattered white specks (gulls/litter/petals) break it up.
- **Cricket Hill** rises in the mid-distance with **three kite flyers** on the crest,
  their kites up on strings. That reads well and is the only content in frame.
- Behind it: the LSD viaduct/fence line and faint peach-toned buildings on the
  horizon under the dusk sky.
- The bike path itself is clean and correct — the problem is everything beside it.

Roughly: it looks like a golf fairway, not a Chicago lakefront park on a summer
evening.

## Why the existing work did not cover it

Task **121** (trail micro-discovery) explicitly measured and dressed the "just
walking" stretches — but its seven waypoints swept the **harbor rim, spit spine,
golf corridor, bay connector and Montrose beach back-edge**. The **big interior
lawns between the bike path and Cricket Hill** were not in that set. So the audit's
own finding — dense cores are fine, connectors degrade to just-walking — is still
true here, in the largest open area in the game.

The Belmont precedent is the model: the *lawnlife dead-space pack* filled Belmont's
empty grass with ~35 bumpable chibis doing real things (picnics, birthday, yoga,
reading, guitar, cornhole, toddler + geese, market). Montrose never got its
equivalent.

## The honest constraint

**Some openness at Montrose is real** and must survive. Montrose Point genuinely is
a huge open green, and the hill is a kite hill precisely because the fetch is open.
The fix is not to carpet it in props.

## OWNER RULING (2026-08-02) — how to fill it

Asked directly, the owner gave the direction himself:

> *"expand the piping plover reserve so we use more of the negative space, adjust
> the other features in montrose harbor so it makes sense with it"*

So the empty lawn is absorbed by a **bigger Montrose Beach Dunes Natural Area /
piping plover reserve**, not by scattered props — and the surrounding Montrose
features are re-laid-out so the geography still reads true afterward. This is the
right answer and a real one: the actual dunes natural area has grown over the years,
it is symbolically fenced rather than walled, and it is the most-loved thing at
Montrose because of the nesting plovers. It also converts dead lawn into something
with a reason to exist.

Full direction in **task 129**.
